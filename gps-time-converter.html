GNSS Time Scale Reference Theory

GPS Time (GPST)

GPS Time is a continuous atomic time scale without leap seconds. It started at 00:00:00 UTC on Sunday, January 6, 1980. Because UTC occasionally implements leap seconds to remain synchronized with Earth's rotation (UT1), UTC has fallen behind GPS time.

  • Leap Seconds: GPS is currently 18 seconds ahead of UTC (as of Jan 1, 2017).
  • Rollovers: The GPS week count is stored as a 10-bit binary value, rolling over every 1024 weeks (19.6 years). Rollovers occurred in August 1999 and April 2019.

Other GNSS Scales

Global navigation satellite systems operate on varying time scales designed to avoid discontinuities:

  • Galileo System Time (GST): Continuous atomic time starting on August 22, 1999. It is synchronized with GPS time (modulo minor nanosecond offsets).
  • BeiDou Time (BDT): Continuous atomic time starting on January 1, 2006. It maintains a constant offset of 14 seconds behind GPS time (BDT = GPS - 14s).
  • GLONASS Time: Runs on Russian UTC+3 (Moscow time) and, unlike other GNSS systems, includes leap seconds, which means GLONASS receivers must handle the occasional 1-second step adjustment.
